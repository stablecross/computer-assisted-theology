**Stage 1 — Formal logic**

With the paper’s explicit exegetical premises stipulated, the principal inferences are valid.

**Exclusion.** Let \(D(x)\) mean Christ died for \(x\), and \(E(x)\) mean \(x\) is elect. Limited atonement asserts:

\[
\forall x\,[D(x)\rightarrow E(x)].
\]

A person for whom Christ died who finally perishes contradicts that proposition, given the stated meaning of election to glory. This establishes exclusion’s falsity without establishing universal extent.

**Extent.** Both lines of Route A work if their personal-content premises are granted. If God commands every lawful hearer to receive as true Christ’s prior giving for that hearer, and God commands no false belief, the giving must be true of every lawful hearer.

Likewise, Route B works if its stated domains are granted: an atoning sacrifice for every person’s sins, or a ransom for every person, reaches every person. Step 21 formally extends the conclusion if its interpretation of Romans 5:18 is stipulated.

The question whether those domains and commanded contents are warranted belongs to Stage 2.

**Bridge.** Step 2 correctly identifies an invalid inference:

\[
S(x)\rightarrow D(x)
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\neg S(x)\rightarrow\neg D(x).
\]

A model in which Christ died for everyone preserves every positive statement that he died for the sheep, church, or elect. Additional exclusionary warrant is therefore necessary.

The paper also correctly distinguishes two different results:

- Showing that an argument lacks its needed premise defeats that proof.
- Producing a purchased person who perishes refutes a universal purchase-to-salvation premise.

**Proclamation.** Part five’s reasoning works if its three tests are stipulated as necessary conditions of what saving faith can receive. Limited atonement cannot authorize the unconditional announcement of Christ’s personal giving for every unknown hearer.

But the necessity of those tests—especially the third—is an asserted exegetical requirement. Formal validity does not establish its warrant.

**Stage 1 verdict: Formally valid, with the paper’s explicitly asserted interpretive premises stipulated.** No central conclusion is defeated here by theological consequences or by denying a stipulated reading.

**1. The exclusion claim: the necessary premises should be accepted**

The noun’s use for the Father elsewhere establishes its range; it does not establish that referent here. [Bible Study Tools](https://www.biblestudytools.com/nrs/jude/1.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Identifying the purchase as Christ’s purchase by death is also **strong canonical synthesis**, rather than a direct statement of the price in 2 Peter.

The strongest objection is the real acquisition of a covenant people modeled on the exodus. The paper properly grants that acquisition need not guarantee final salvation. Its strongest answer is that the corresponding Christian acquisition is expressly associated with Christ’s blood in 1 Peter.

The strongest rejoinder is:

> First Peter names the blood-price of believers. That does not automatically identify every acquisition metaphor applied to false teachers.

That rejoinder correctly prevents a mechanical transfer of the believer population into 2 Peter 2:1. The paper does not make that transfer: Peter independently predicates purchase of the teachers. The synthesis identifies the purchase’s character.

Using the criteria’s classifications:

- Real acquisition followed by destruction in the exodus is **(b), independently warranted**.
- Applying that pattern to Christian acquisition by Christ’s blood has positive warrant in the Petrine correspondence.
- A further purchase of these teachers by Christ, distinct from his blood-purchase, lacks evidence establishing that specific relation. General divine ownership does not establish it. That indispensable rescue is **(c), merely conceivable**.
- Reducing “bought them” to their profession likewise supplies an unmarked qualifier.

The blood-purchase reading therefore has sufficient positive warrant to accept it. The exclusion claim survives the strongest identified objection and rejoinder.

Hebrews 10:29 strengthens the case but does not independently settle every question about sanctification. The import expressly treats its reading as weighed rather than conclusively established; atonement.md preserves that limitation.

John 11 supplies further corroboration, with one qualification: dying for a nation considered collectively does not, by itself, settle every distributive claim about its individual members. That qualification does not affect the principal purchase proof.

**First John 2:2.** The move from “ours” to “the whole world” is directly expansive. Reading it through 5:19 is a **same-letter identification** supported by the repeated contrast between God’s children and the world.

The strongest rival is a geographically worldwide redeemed people, rather than every individual. But the geographical breadth of redemption does not establish that restrictive relation in this passage. The opponent needs warrant for replacing the world beyond the believing company with a selected portion of that world. The repeated contrast supplies stronger warrant for the paper’s reading. One subsidiary sentence should be narrowed. John 17:9 does not make “world” incapable of including persons who subsequently believe. The prayer mentions people given **from** the world and later believers. Nevertheless, that correction leaves the same-letter argument from 1 John 2 and 5 intact.

**First Timothy 2:1–6.** The paper’s continuity argument is strong. Prayer, God’s saving desire, the one mediator, and the ransom belong to one connected argument.

The strongest “all kinds” reading correctly observes the emphasis on rulers and Gentiles. But those markers establish inclusion across ranks and peoples. They do not establish exclusion of individuals within those ranks and peoples. Maintaining one restricted domain throughout avoids a partition between occurrences of “all”; it still needs positive warrant for the restriction itself. Thus both principal texts provide substantial independent warrant for the universal human scope of the apostolic gospel.

The supporting texts remain correctly subordinate:

- “Especially believers” in 1 Timothy 4:10 distinguishes believers within a broader class, while the relevant sense of “Savior” remains contestable.
- Hebrews 2:9 warrants serious consideration but carries the contextual domain question the paper acknowledges.
- Second Corinthians 5:14–15 distinguishes descriptions—those who died and those who live—but those descriptions alone do not prove unequal populations. The paper overstates what that wording independently establishes. Its principal Route B survives this local correction.

**3. Route A: the necessary personal-content premises are not established**

Acts 13 establishes that forgiveness through Jesus is proclaimed to a mixed audience. It also expressly identifies believing as the condition of receiving release. These are direct statements.

What it does not independently establish is that the proclamation asserts to each unbeliever an unconditional historical proposition about Christ’s having died for that individual’s sins. The paper’s own concession in step 54 recognizes the difference between that assertion and a personally applicable conditional promise. [Bible Study Tools](https://www.biblestudytools.com/nrs/acts/13.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

First Corinthians 15 strongly establishes that Christ’s death for sins belongs to the gospel delivered before its reception. The weaker move is assigning “our” an exhaustively inclusive reference to every original unbelieving listener. Paul’s present address identifies a company that received and stands in the gospel. The passage does not independently specify the disputed reference of the pronoun at every earlier proclamation.

This objection grants that the death-for-sins message preceded faith. It challenges the widening from that datum to the individual inclusion of every original listener.

Galatians 2:20 directly establishes Christ’s giving for Paul and Paul’s present faith in that Christ. Connecting it with 2:16 establishes continuity with justifying faith. It does not establish that every person beginning to believe must first know the proposition “Christ gave himself specifically for me.”

The paper’s strongest answer is the giving’s priority to believing. The strongest rejoinder distinguishes two relations:

1. Christ’s giving for Paul preceded Paul’s faith.
2. Paul knew that personal relation before beginning to trust Christ.

The first does not establish the second. Recognizing a prior act through faith does not make faith cause that act. Nor does allowing knowledge to develop require two different kinds of saving faith. Galatians describes the life Paul **now** lives; it does not prescribe the complete epistemic sequence of every conversion. Consequently, neither line of Route A completes its offered proof independently of Route B.

**4. The extension before the cross: retrospective efficacy is established; retrospective universal extent is not**

Hebrews 9:15 establishes that Christ’s death reaches transgressions under the first covenant. It therefore defeats a chronological barrier to the death’s efficacy. It does not identify every deceased human as someone for whom that death occurred. [biblestudytools.com](https://www.biblestudytools.com/nrs/hebrews/9.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Romans 5:18 supplies the proposed universal bridge. Its paired “all” expressions favor the paper’s reading, but the relationship established by the parallel must be specified.

The surrounding data concern:

- those receiving the gift in verse 17;
- justification and life in verse 18;
- people being made righteous in verse 19.

The strongest rival reads the parallel as contrasting the consequences of the two representative acts for their respective populations. Its recipient distinction has positive contextual warrant; it is not introduced merely because restricted domains are possible.

The paper’s provision-and-reception distinction has independent warrant. What remains unproved is its application here: that the second “all” quantifies everyone for whom the act occurred, including deceased unbelievers, while its stated justification-and-life result applies only to recipients.

The Stage 3 comparison with 1 Corinthians 15:22–23 reinforces this finding: a Pauline Adam–Christ parallel can use paired “all” expressions while positively identifying the Christ-related population. This does not automatically dictate Romans’ domain, but it defeats the claim that the parallel itself establishes identical populations. **5. The bridge and proclamation claims**

The specified limited-atonement arguments generally do require the additional warrant the paper identifies. Romans 8:32 is the strongest challenge: the giving of the Son grounds further gifts. The paper’s answer survives because the passage positively identifies the company whose assurance is being defended. Turning that assurance into a rule for every object of Christ’s death requires further warrant.

Several defenses should nevertheless be narrower:

- Accomplishment and reception are distinguishable; mentioning both does not alone establish universal provision.
- Different results of the cross do not independently establish a complete account of God’s intentions.
- The old covenant’s mixed population does not prove that every feature of that population carries unchanged into the new covenant.

Those corrections leave the central demand for an exclusion or saving bridge intact.

For proclamation, **step 54’s promise is not a landed counterexample to the paper’s third test**. The paper already grants its truth and its knowability. Repeating it does not answer the demand for a known personal historical ground.

The landed objection concerns the **necessity of that demand**. The paper has not established that saving faith requires prior knowledge of individual inclusion in the death. Galatians 2:20 does not supply that requirement, and part five’s candidate tests cannot supply their own biblical warrant.

The narrower negative survives: limited atonement cannot authorize the unconditional statement “Christ died for you” to every unknown hearer. The paper has not shown that this restriction eliminates everything saving faith could receive.

The positive apostolic content remains strong. Independently of Route A, Route B supplies apostolic testimony to an atoning sacrifice for the world and a ransom for all. It provides a basis for personal proclamation to the gospel’s human hearers.

**Stage 2 verdict:** accept the necessary exclusion premises and Route B’s broad human domain. Do not accept the offered warrants for Route A’s stronger initial-belief requirement or step 21’s extension to every deceased person before the cross. The specified bridge criticisms survive, with the local corrections stated above.
