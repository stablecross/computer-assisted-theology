# abrahamic-covenant

What God's covenant with Abraham gives, and how far it reaches.

## What this is

The covenant with Abraham gives five things.

1. **God guarantees the inheritance himself.** When the covenant is made, only God passes between the pieces (Gen 15:17–18). He calls the land "a perpetual holding" (17:8) and confirms the promise by an oath (22:16; Heb 6:13–18).
2. **God chooses which children inherit.** Isaac, not Ishmael (Gen 17:19–21; 21:12). Jacob, not Esau (25:23).
3. **The chosen children are born by divine intervention.** Sarah was barren (Gen 11:30; 18:11), and so was Rebekah until the LORD granted Isaac's prayer (25:21). Paul calls Isaac "the child who was born according to the Spirit" (Gal 4:29); compare John 3:3.
4. **The inheritance of the chosen children is announced before they are born.** Isaac is named before he is conceived (Gen 17:19). Jacob's place over his brother is announced while the twins are in the womb (25:23), and Paul reads that as "God's purpose of election" (Rom 9:11).
5. **The inheritance is the eschatological kingdom of heaven, and it comes through resurrection.** Abraham received none of the land "as a heritage, not even a foot's length" (Acts 7:5). The only ground he owned there was a tomb (Gen 23). Hebrews says why: "he looked forward to the city that has foundations, whose architect and builder is God" (11:10), and he "considered the fact that God is able even to raise someone from the dead" (11:19). Jesus places Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob "in the kingdom of heaven" (Matt 8:11). And Hebrews says that all the heirs receive it together: "they would not, apart from us, be made perfect" (11:40).

Points 2 through 4 are narrated in Genesis and are hard to contest from the immediate context. Points 1 and 5 are argued. Point 1 has to answer the obedience clauses at Genesis 22:16 and 26:5, and point 5 rests on how Hebrews, Acts, and Jesus read Genesis.

A reading that holds in one place must also hold everywhere else in Scripture. The criteria call this global propagation. So the papers do not stop at Genesis. They test these points against the rest of the Old and New Testaments, and they follow them where the rest of Scripture takes them.

## The papers

Read them in this order. A later paper may import the conclusion of an earlier one, and it names the paper it imports from.

1. **[Election in the Abrahamic Covenant Is to Salvation](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology/blob/main/abrahamic-covenant/election-to-salvation.md)**
   Claim: the election of the Abrahamic promise-heir class, with Isaac as its founding case and those Paul places in his category as its later members, is election to salvation.
   Imports: nothing.

2. **[The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology/blob/main/abrahamic-covenant/election-not-grounded-in-man.md)**
   Claim: that election is not conditioned on works and does not rest on the man's willing; for faith, actual or foreseen, the reading with no human criterion is better supported.
   Imports: paper 1, that the election is to salvation.

3. **[God Brings Every Heir to Faith and Loses None of Them](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology/blob/main/abrahamic-covenant/heirs-brought-and-kept.md)**
   Claims: every heir of the promise is brought to faith by God's act; God's regenerating act does not fail in anyone to whom it is given; no one who is justified finally falls away.
   Imports: papers 1 and 2, and "No One Comes to Christ Unless God First Changes the Heart" in [ezekiel/](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology/tree/main/ezekiel).
