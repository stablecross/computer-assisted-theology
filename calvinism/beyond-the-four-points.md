**What this is.** [calvinism.md](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology/blob/main/calvinism/calvinism.md) states four claims about outcomes. The Canons of Dort (1619) and the Westminster Confession of Faith (1646) assert more than those four claims. This document lists what they assert beyond what calvinism.md establishes. For each item it says why the papers have not taken it up.

**This is not an argument.** It makes no claim that any item is false. A conclusion that has not reached the bar the criteria set is not shown false. It is only not shown. The top-level [README](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology#how-strict-the-criteria-are) explains the bar.

**References.** The Canons of Dort are cited by head and article: "Dort I.9" is the First Head of Doctrine, article 9. The third and fourth heads are one section, cited as III/IV. The Westminster Confession is cited by chapter and section: "WCF 6.3" is chapter 6, section 3. Each confessional claim is paraphrased, with a few short phrases quoted.

## Why an item has not been taken up

There are two reasons, and most items have both.

**Priority.** calvinism.md establishes four outcome claims: who can come, whom God chooses, who believes, and who is kept. An item that those claims do not need waits its turn. Some items are bracketed on purpose. calvinism.md does not argue how the condition from Adam passes to each person, or how grace acts on the will.

**Difficulty of proof under the criteria.** Each item is given one of three grades, or a fourth when the claim itself is unclear.

- **Within reach.** The item follows from the conclusions of calvinism.md with one further step, and a text states that step directly. Example: calvinism.md shows that God's decision to regenerate secures faith, justification, and keeping. 2 Thessalonians 2:13 joins God's choosing to sanctification, which carries the decision to sanctification as well.
- **Open.** A route exists, but one load-bearing premise has no text that states it, or a rival reading has warrant that the argument would have to exclude. Example: Romans 5:18 says that one man's trespass led to condemnation for all. Whether that is guilt imputed directly or condemnation through an inherited condition is a question the verse does not settle.
- **Doubtful.** The item needs a premise that Scripture does not appear to state at all. Usually it is a definition, a mechanism, or an arrangement the text does not declare. Under the evidential rule, such a premise is unavailable until someone shows a text that supplies it. Example: Genesis 2 has a command, a penalty, and the tree of life, but no text states the Confession's terms for a covenant of works: life for Adam's posterity on condition of perfect obedience.
- **Ambiguous.** The confessional wording can be read as more than one claim, and the readings differ on the point in dispute. Until the claim is fixed, there is nothing to grade.

A grade is a judgment about the difficulty of a proof. It is not a finding that the proof fails.

## Total depravity

calvinism.md shows the following. No one comes to Christ unless God first changes his heart. The condition that keeps a man from coming is native to every man, Christ excepted. It comes from Adam, by a same-author identification and by an argument within Romans. It is found in the heart, the will, the mind, and the understanding.

**T1. Original righteousness, holiness, and ability to obey** (Dort III/IV.1; WCF 4.2, 6.2, 9.2). *Doubtful.* The usual route runs from the image of God (Gen 1:27) and God's verdict that creation is very good (1:31). The image of God is undefined. Goodness is likewise undefined: in the same account, God says it is not good for the man to be alone (2:18). A command to Adam does not show that he could keep it.

Three other texts are offered. God made the man *yāšār*, straight, "but they have devised many schemes" (Eccl 7:29). The new self is "created according to the likeness of God in true righteousness and holiness" (Eph 4:24), and it is renewed in knowledge according to the image of its creator (Col 3:10). These support an original uprightness. None of them states an ability to obey, and the Confession's claim includes one: Adam had "freedom and power to will and to do that which is good" (WCF 9.2).

**T2. Imputed guilt** (WCF 6.3). *Open.* Romans 5:18 says one man's trespass led to condemnation for all. The Confession says more: Adam's guilt itself is reckoned to each descendant. A rival reading has the condemnation come through the condition each person inherits. The argument would also have to answer Ezekiel 18:20, which says a child does not bear a parent's iniquity. It would have to show that Adam stands to his descendants as more than a parent.

**T3. Transmission by ordinary generation** (Dort III/IV.2; WCF 6.3). *Open.* calvinism.md already has the condition native to every man and coming from Adam. What this item adds is that ordinary generation is how it passes. Texts tie the condition to birth and descent: Adam fathers a son in his likeness (Gen 5:3); no one brings a clean thing out of an unclean (Job 14:4); the psalmist was sinful from conception (Ps 51:5); what is born of the flesh is flesh (John 3:6). Each falls short of the step. Psalm 51:5 speaks of one man. John uses "flesh" for what the Word became (John 1:14), so "what is born of the flesh is flesh" does not by itself name a corruption, though the passage does say that everyone born must be born from above (3:3, 7). Luke 1:35 ties Christ's holiness to the Spirit's overshadowing. It does not say what ordinary conception passes on.

**T4. Every faculty and part** (Dort III/IV.1; WCF 6.2). *Doubtful as stated.* "Every" needs a text that covers every faculty, by a list or by a rule. Scripture names several, and calvinism.md gathers the heart, the will, the mind, and the understanding. No text gives a rule that reaches the rest. A claim that the whole person is affected is open.

**T5. All actual sins proceed from the corruption** (WCF 6.4). *Open.* Mark 7:21–23 traces evil intentions and a list of evil acts to the heart. James 1:14–15 traces sin to a person's own desire. Whether every transgression proceeds from a corruption received from Adam is the open step.

**T6. Corruption remains in the regenerate, and its motions are sin** (WCF 6.5). *Open.* That sin remains in believers is direct (1 John 1:8; Gal 5:17). Romans 7:7 makes a desire, coveting, a breach of the law. Romans 7:14–25 describes sin that dwells in the one speaking. If the speaker is regenerate, that carries the claim. Who the speaker is is disputed, and the rival readings have warrant: compare 7:14 with 6:18. James 1:15 has desire conceive and then give birth to sin, which a rival reads as a line between desire and sin.

**T7. The good works of the unregenerate are sinful** (WCF 16.7). *Open.* Those in the flesh cannot please God (Rom 8:8), and without faith it is impossible to please him (Heb 11:6). That is not yet that their works are sinful. No One Comes grants that the unregenerate do civil good. Romans 14:23 says "whatever does not proceed from faith is sin." Its context is a believer acting against his own conviction about food, and the argument would have to show that the sentence reaches every act of the unregenerate.

## Unconditional election

calvinism.md shows the following. Election is to salvation, has persons as its objects, comes before anything they do, and is not conditioned on anything they do, faith included. For other responses, Scripture gives none as a condition. Whether something a man is, rather than something he does, bears on his election is left open.

**U1. Nothing in the creature** (Dort I.9; WCF 3.5). *Open.* calvinism.md excludes what a man does. The confessions also exclude what he is. Deuteronomy 7:7 excludes one property: Israel was not chosen for being numerous. 1 Corinthians 1:26–29 says God chose the weak and the low. The argument would have to show that such properties serve God's purpose without being conditions of a person's election.

**U2. Good pleasure alone** (Dort I.10). *Open on the first reading; within reach on the second.* That election comes from God's good pleasure is direct (Eph 1:5, 9, 11). "Alone" can be taken two ways. Dort I.10 takes it to exclude any human quality as a condition, and then U2 is U1. It can also mean that every property God might weigh is itself from his good pleasure. Then it follows from B4 by the argument of calvinism.md step 5: a rule that names a property God put there names nothing in the man that God did not put there. On that reading U2 follows from B4's first part, which is within reach.

**U3. Election appoints the means** (Dort I.9; WCF 3.6). *Within reach.* calvinism.md shows that the decision to regenerate secures faith, justification, and keeping. 2 Thessalonians 2:13 joins God's choosing to sanctification. "All the means" reaches every circumstance that leads to salvation. Romans 8:28 says that "all things work together for good for those who love God, who are called according to his purpose," and Lamentations 3:37–38 puts every event under God's ordaining (B4).

**U4. A fixed number; an unchangeable election** (Dort I.7, I.11; WCF 3.4). *Within reach.* calvinism.md shows that the saved are exactly the regenerated, and it identifies the decision to regenerate as the election. So the elect and the saved are the same persons. The Confession says more: the number "cannot be either increased or diminished." Hebrews 6:17 speaks of "the heirs of the promise" and "the unchangeable character of his purpose." The election calvinism.md establishes is the election of the promise-heir class.

**U5. Reprobation** (Dort I.15; WCF 3.7). *Within reach for the Confession; Doubtful for the Canons.* calvinism.md shows that God does not regenerate everyone, and that whom he regenerates is his decision. The Confession says that God was pleased "to pass by, and to ordain them to dishonour and wrath for their sin." To pass a man by is not to regenerate him. Not regenerating him is part of the decision that elects others, so the passing by is as early as the election. Those passed by are condemned for their own sins (John 3:18–19). Romans 9:22 states the purpose: God, "desiring to show his wrath and to make known his power." The Canons say more. God leaves the rest "in the common misery into which they have wilfully plunged themselves." That is a fall from a state man could have kept, and so it needs T1's ability to obey.

## Irresistible grace

calvinism.md shows the following. Everyone to whom God gives the regenerating act comes to faith. It brackets how the act works on the will.

**I1. Passive in regeneration** (WCF 10.2). *Open.* John 1:13 says the children of God are born of God, not of human will. That is direct about the source of the birth. "Altogether passive" says more: the man contributes nothing within the act. That is a claim about how the act works.

**I2. How the will is renewed** (Dort III/IV.11–12, 16). *Doubtful for infusion and healing; Ambiguous for non-coercion.* Philippians 2:13 says that God works the willing and the working of believers. It does not say how. Infused qualities and healing are accounts of how. That grace does not coerce is a claim about the outcome: what God works is the willing itself, and "your people will offer themselves willingly" (Ps 110:3). Whether a willing God works is uncoerced depends on what coercion means. The Canons do not define it, so the claim is not fixed.

**I3. Freedom restored; immutable good in glory** (WCF 9.4–5). *Ambiguous.* The Confession uses "free" in two senses. In 9.1 the will's liberty is that it is "neither forced, nor by any absolute necessity of nature determined, to good or evil." In 9.2 Adam had "freedom and power to will and to do that which is good," but "mutably." In the second sense, 9.4 is Romans 6:18: God "freeth him from his natural bondage under sin." And a will "immutably free to good alone" (9.5) has a power to will good that cannot be lost. In the first sense, the same phrase contradicts itself, since a will that can will good alone and cannot change is determined to good. Until "free" is fixed to one sense, there is nothing to grade.

## Perseverance of the saints

calvinism.md shows the following. No one who is justified finally falls away. It does not claim that a believer cannot fall totally for a time.

**P1. No total fall, even for a time** (Dort V.6; WCF 11.5, 17.1). *Open.* Toward it: Christ prays that Peter's faith not fail (Luke 22:32), and God's seed abides (1 John 3:9). Needing an account: those who have fallen away from grace (Gal 5:4), and the shipwreck of faith (1 Tim 1:19). Dort V.6 says what persists through a fall: the grace of adoption and the state of justification. calvinism.md shows that no justified man finally falls away. That none loses justification for a time is the open step.

**P2. Certain renewal to repentance** (Dort V.7). *Open.* A justified man who falls is not finally lost, and discipline aims at keeping believers from condemnation (Heb 12:5–11; 1 Cor 11:32). The claim needs a further premise: either that final salvation requires repentance for each grievous fall, or a promise that God renews a believer after each fall.

**P3. Assurance** (Dort V.9–11; WCF 18.1–4). *Doubtful.* Believers may know that they have eternal life (1 John 5:13). The Confession claims more: an "infallible assurance of faith" (WCF 18.2). It also says that hypocrites "may vainly deceive themselves with false hopes" (WCF 18.1). Jesus describes such men. They say "Lord, Lord," and point to prophecy, exorcisms, and deeds of power done in his name. He answers, "I never knew you" (Matt 7:22–23). So a certain assurance needs a mark by which a man can tell his own assurance from a false hope. Scripture states no such mark. A mark stated in a text would not settle it either. The man would still have to judge from the inside that he bears the mark, and the deceived man makes the same judgment. The dreamer has the same problem: nothing he can check from inside the dream shows that he is awake.

**P4. The sacraments as means** (Dort V.14; WCF 14.1). *Open.* Texts connect baptism and the Supper with Christ and with salvation (Rom 6:3–4; 1 Cor 10:16). Peter says baptism "now saves you—not as a removal of dirt from the body, but as an appeal to God for a good conscience" (1 Pet 3:21). What that qualification leaves to the sign is the open step. How the sacraments serve as means belongs to ecclesiology.

## Outside the four points

**B1. The covenant of works** (WCF 7.2). *Doubtful.* Genesis 2 has a command, a penalty, and the tree of life. The covenant ceremony, sign, and seal are unspecified. Their absence alone does not decide the question, since 2 Samuel 7 has none of them and Scripture still names the arrangement with David a covenant (2 Sam 23:5; Ps 89:3). A covenant can be shown by inference as well as by name, but then the relation has to be argued. Hosea 6:7 would name it only if it refers to Adam the man, and the NRSV reads it as a place. Genesis 3:22–24 ties the tree to continued life. What no text states is the Confession's terms: life promised to Adam, "and in him to his posterity, upon condition of perfect and personal obedience." That is the missing premise.

**B2. Infants** (WCF 10.3; Dort I.17). *Within reach for the Confession; Doubtful for the Canons.* The Confession's claim is conditional: if an infant is elect and dies in infancy, it is regenerated and saved. Election is to salvation, so an elect infant is saved. No one sees the kingdom of God without being born from above (John 3:3), so a saved infant is regenerated. The Canons claim more: believing parents should not doubt the election of their children who die in infancy. That picks out the elect by a mark, being the child of a believer. Abraham was a believing parent. Both of his sons received the covenant sign (Gen 17:23–26), but God established his covenant with Isaac and not with Ishmael (17:19–21). Paul draws the rule from that case: not all of Abraham's children are his descendants (Rom 9:7–8). That defeats the general inference from a believing parent, or from the covenant sign, to election. It does not reach Dort's narrower class, since Ishmael lived to old age (Gen 25:17). For that class the text offered is 1 Corinthians 7:14, which calls the children of a believer holy. It says the same of an unbelieving spouse, whose salvation 7:16 leaves uncertain. So "holy" does not carry election, and no other text is offered for children who die in infancy.

**B3. Limited atonement** (Dort II.8; WCF 8.5, 8.8). *Ambiguous.* Members of both assemblies held two incompatible views of the extent of the atonement, for whom Christ died. One view was that Christ died only for the elect. The other was that Christ died for everyone, and that only the elect are saved. At Dort, men who held the second view signed the Canons. At Westminster, men who held it took part in the Assembly that passed the Confession. What was agreed is the words, and the words can be read either way. The Canons set the two readings side by side. II.3 calls Christ's death "of infinite worth and value, abundantly sufficient to expiate the sins of the whole world." II.8 says it was God's will that Christ "should effectually redeem... all those, and those only, who were from eternity chosen to salvation." Both readings keep the particular saving efficacy II.8 states. They differ on whether its "only" excludes any intention of the death toward the non-elect. Who signed is background, not the proof. [atonement.md](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology/blob/main/calvinism/atonement.md) takes up the question.

**B4. The universal decree** (WCF 3.1). *Within reach that God ordains whatever comes to pass; Open for "unchangeably"; Ambiguous for "not the author of sin" and for no violence to the will; Doubtful for second causes.* "Who can command and have it done, if the Lord has not ordained it? Is it not from the mouth of the Most High that good and bad come?" (Lam 3:37–38). God "accomplishes all things according to his counsel and will" (Eph 1:11), "declaring the end from the beginning" (Isa 46:10). Texts assign one event both to God's purpose and to men who acted willingly and wickedly (Gen 50:20; Acts 4:27–28).

Jeremiah 32:35 does not stand against this. "Nor did it enter my mind that they should do this abomination" cannot mean that God never conceived of the act, since he had forbidden it by name (Lev 18:21; Deut 12:31). It denies that the act was his command or his approval.

"Unchangeably" has rival texts. God is not a mortal "that he should change his mind" (Num 23:19), and yet "God changed his mind about the calamity" he had announced for Nineveh (Jonah 3:10). Jeremiah 18:8 makes such an announcement conditional: if the nation turns from its evil, "I will change my mind about the disaster that I intended to bring on it." Whether every text in which God changes his mind fits that pattern is the open step.

The Confession adds that God is not "the author of sin," and that the liberty or contingency of second causes is not taken away but established. It glosses the first: the sinfulness "proceedeth only from the creature, and not from God" (WCF 5.4). That can be read two ways. Read as responsibility, it says the evil intent is the creature's and he answers for it. The texts state that: Joseph's brothers intended harm, and God intended the same event for good (Gen 50:20). Read as source, it says the sin comes from somewhere other than the one who ordained it. No text states that, and Herod and Pilate did what God's hand and plan had predestined (Acts 4:27–28). Until the clause is fixed to one reading, there is nothing to grade. The clause about second causes needs an account of primary and secondary causes. That account is contested, and no text supplies it. The Confession also says that no violence is offered to the will of creatures. That is the claim of I2, and it is Ambiguous for the same reason.

## An invitation

Anyone who wants to establish one of these items under the criteria is welcome to try. Write it as a paper. State its claim, list its imports, number its steps, and submit it to review under the [criteria](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology/blob/main/criteria.md). The same rules bind it that bind every paper in this repository.

A grade can also be wrong. Citing a text is not enough to show that. A text can be placed beside a claim without supporting it. The argument that a text supplies a missing premise has to meet the criteria: the text read in its context, and that reading tested against the rest of Scripture. An argument that does is a bug report, and the [top-level README](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology#how-to-report-a-bug) says how to file one.
