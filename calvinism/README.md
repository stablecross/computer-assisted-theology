# calvinism

What the papers in [abrahamic-covenant/](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology/tree/main/abrahamic-covenant) and [ezekiel/](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology/tree/main/ezekiel) establish about four points of Calvinism.

## What this is

The papers in abrahamic-covenant/ and ezekiel/ argue from Genesis, Ezekiel, and the rest of Scripture. Their conclusions supply the substance of four points of Calvinism: total depravity, unconditional election, irresistible grace, and the perseverance of the saints.

calvinism.md joins those conclusions and states each point as a claim about outcomes: who can come, whom God chooses, who believes, and who is kept.

## Which way the argument runs

A common objection says that Calvinism exists only because Calvinists read Scripture through the Calvinist system. Applied to these papers, that objection is a claim that can be checked: somewhere, a step uses the system as a premise.

Four facts make the check easy to run.

- **Every paper lists its imports at the top.** The papers in abrahamic-covenant/ and ezekiel/ import only from one another.
- **None of them imports calvinism.md.** The dependencies run one way. The papers argue from the text, and calvinism.md comes last and draws on them.
- **Confessions are never premises.** [corpus.md](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology/blob/main/corpus.md) says so. In the papers, the Canons of Dort and the Westminster Confession appear only in closing sections, to report where a conclusion agrees with them.
- **Doctrine names appear only where no step uses them.** A paper names a doctrine in its closing section, as a label for what it has already argued, or in its front matter, as a question it sets aside. No step depends on the name.

So the objection has a precise form. Name the step whose premise is a doctrine rather than a text, or an inference that holds only if the system is assumed. That is a bug report, and the [top-level README](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology#how-to-report-a-bug) says how to file one.

## The paper

1. **[Four Points of Calvinism: What the Papers Establish](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology/blob/main/calvinism/calvinism.md)**
   Claims: no one comes to Christ unless God first changes his heart, and the condition is native, from Adam, and found in the heart, will, mind, and understanding; election is to salvation and is not conditioned on anything the man does, faith included; everyone to whom God gives the regenerating act comes to faith; no one who is justified finally falls away.
   Imports: all four papers in abrahamic-covenant/ and ezekiel/.
   Adds: the argument that completes the case for faith, which "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man" leaves comparative; and two syntheses under total depravity, Adam as the source of the condition and the parts of the man it is found in.

## What the papers do not show

[Beyond the Four Points](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology/blob/main/calvinism/beyond-the-four-points.md) lists what the Canons of Dort and the Westminster Confession assert beyond calvinism.md. For each item it says why the papers have not taken it up: priority, and how hard the item would be to prove under the criteria. It is not an argument, and it claims no item is false.

## Not argued

The fifth point, limited atonement. How grace acts on the will: Scripture says that God works in believers' willing (Phil 2:13), but not how. How the condition from Adam passes to each person. Whether a believer can fall totally for a time, short of finally.
