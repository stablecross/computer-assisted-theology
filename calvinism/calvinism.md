Each import is argued in its own paper. A defect in an import is a defect in the paper that argues it, and any claim here that rests on the import falls with it.

- Believers belong to that class (steps 9 through 12).

- Romans 8:29–30 predicates an antecedent predestining of persons and carries the same persons to glory (step 11).
- The election is not conditioned on works, and it does not rest on the man's willing or running (steps 1 through 7).
- No responsive candidate other than faith has a text making it the condition of election, so under the evidential rule none is available (step 2a).
- For faith, the reading with no human criterion is better supported, and the rival is not shown unavailable there (steps 2b, 5a, and the conclusion).

**From "[No One Comes to Christ Unless God First Changes the Heart](https://github.com/stablecross/computer-assisted-theology/blob/main/ezekiel/no-one-comes.md)."**

- Production: where the response occurs, God's act brought it about (front matter; step 5).
- The condition is every man's natural condition (step 10).
- No one is good (closing section).

- The act claim: every instance of the regenerating act yields faith (step 9).
- The keeping claim: no one who is justified finally falls away (step 17), by three routes, Romans 8 (step 13), Romans 5:9–10 (step 14), and Philippians 1:6 and 2:12–13 (step 15).
- A reading of a warning on which a justified man is finally lost is not available once it is held with Romans 5:9–10, Philippians 1:6, and Philippians 2:13 (section on warnings).
- Not all come to life (John 5:28–29; Dan 12:2), a fact that paper uses and adds nothing to.

---

**Claim under review.** Four claims, each about an outcome.

- **Total depravity.** No one comes to Christ unless God first changes his heart. The condition that keeps a man from coming is native to every man, Christ excepted (Luke 1:35; John 8:29), comes from Adam, and is found in the heart, the will, the mind, and the understanding.
- **Unconditional election.** God's election is to salvation, it has persons as its objects, it comes before anything they do, and it is not conditioned on anything they do. That includes their faith, actual or foreseen. For responses other than works and faith, the claim is that Scripture gives none as a condition. Whether something a man is, rather than something he does, bears on his election is left open.
- **Irresistible grace.** Everyone to whom God gives the regenerating act comes to faith.
- **Perseverance of the saints.** No one who is justified finally falls away.

**What this paper does.** Most of it joins conclusions already argued in four papers. It adds three things of its own. The first is the argument in the section on unconditional election, which completes the case for faith that "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man" left comparative. The other two join texts in the section on total depravity: Adam as the source of the condition, and the condition gathered across the parts of the man. Everything else is stated from the imports, and each joining of texts is classified where it is made.

**How the doctrines are stated here.** Each doctrine is stated as a claim about outcomes: who can come, whom God chooses and why, who believes, and who is kept. The traditional formulations also give an account of how grace acts on the will. Scripture says that God is at work in the willing of believers — "it is God who is at work in you, enabling you both to will and to work for his good pleasure" (Phil 2:13). The Greek has God working the willing and the working themselves, καὶ τὸ θέλειν καὶ τὸ ἐνεργεῖν, *kai to thelein kai to energein*. The NRSV supplies *enabling*; the objects of God's working are the willing and the working themselves. Scripture does not say how God works them, and this paper brackets the mechanism. The Canons of Dort and the Westminster Confession are named below only to report where the outcomes agree with them. They are not premises.

**Not under review.** For whom Christ died. Why God passes over anyone, and how many. How the will works. How the condition inherited from Adam passes to each person. Whether a property a man does not perform — something he is rather than something he does — bears on his election. Whether a believer can fall totally for a time, short of finally.

## Total depravity

**What is imported.** No One Comes argues that no one comes to Christ unless God first changes his heart. The response does not occur while the heart Ezekiel names remains, and where the response occurs, God's act brought it about. Step 10 of that paper crosses from Ezekiel's audience to every man, on Romans 8:7–8, Ephesians 2:3, 1 Corinthians 2:14, and John 6:44 and 65. Its closing section adds that no one is good: "No one is good but God alone" (Luke 18:19), which Paul states from the Psalter (Rom 3:10–12).

**The condition is native.** Paul says where it comes from in the man: "we were by nature children of wrath, like everyone else" (Eph 2:3). The word is φύσει, *physei*, by nature. Genesis says it is there from youth: "the inclination of the human heart is evil from youth" (Gen 8:21). Jesus says that everyone born must be born again: "no one can see the kingdom of God without being born from above" (John 3:3), and "You must be born from above" (3:7). John uses "flesh" for what the Word became (1:14), so "what is born of the flesh is flesh" (3:6) does not by itself name a corruption. What the passage does say is that natural birth does not supply what seeing the kingdom requires, and that holds of everyone born. David says it of himself: "I was born guilty, a sinner when my mother conceived me" (Ps 51:5). That is one man's case, and it corroborates. So the condition is not merely acquired by habit or example. A man has it as he is. It can also grow worse by practice: those who would not honor God "became futile in their thinking, and their senseless minds were darkened" (Rom 1:21).

**Christ is the exception.** The child Mary bore "will be holy," and the reason given is the Spirit's coming upon her (Luke 1:35). Paul says that "those who are in the flesh cannot please God" (Rom 8:8). Jesus says, "I always do what is pleasing to him" (John 8:29). Hebrews adds that he was tested "as we are, yet without sin" (Heb 4:15).

**The condition comes from Adam.** Paul names the source. "Sin came into the world through one man, and death came through sin, and so death spread to all because all have sinned" (Rom 5:12). "By the one man's disobedience the many were made sinners" (5:19). "All die in Adam" (1 Cor 15:22).

That Adam is the source of sin and death is direct statement. That he is the source of the particular condition No One Comes describes — the heart that will not listen — is a same-author identification. It joins Romans 5:19's *made sinners* to Ephesians 2:3's *by nature*, reading Paul's usage across two letters.

**Romans traces the same sin from Adam to the inability.** Paul follows one noun, ἁμαρτία, *hamartia*, through one argument. Sin "came into the world through one man" (Rom 5:12). Sin "exercised dominion in death" (5:21). The readers were "slaves of sin" (6:17), and "when you were slaves of sin, you were free in regard to righteousness" (6:20). Paul then places the sin in the flesh: "it is no longer I that do it, but sin that dwells within me. For I know that nothing good dwells within me, that is, in my flesh" (7:17–18), and "with my flesh I am a slave to the law of sin" (7:25). God "condemned sin in the flesh" (8:3), and the mind set on the flesh "is hostile to God; it does not submit to God's law—indeed it cannot" (8:7). That last verse is one No One Comes uses for the inability. So the sin that entered through one man is the sin that rules, enslaves, dwells in the flesh, and holds the mind that cannot submit. This route is one writer's argument within one letter. The noun carries it from 5:12 to 8:3. The last link runs through *flesh*, which Paul joins to sin at 7:17–18, 7:25, and 8:3. It does not depend on reading *made sinners* at 5:19 as more than a standing.

**It does not come by copying Adam.** Death reigned "even over those whose sins were not like the transgression of Adam" (Rom 5:14), so death does not depend on repeating his act. And the many were made sinners "by the one man's disobedience" (5:19). The cause Paul names is Adam's act, not their copying of it. Neither verse says how what comes from Adam passes to each person.

**A child does not bear a parent's iniquity — Ezekiel 18:20.** "A child shall not suffer for the iniquity of a parent, nor a parent suffer for the iniquity of a child" (Ezek 18:20; see Deut 24:16). An objector will say this forbids a condition received from Adam. Ezekiel's rule answers the proverb of 18:2, "The parents have eaten sour grapes, and the children's teeth are set on edge." It concerns who is punished for whose deeds. The claim here is not that men are punished for Adam's deed. It is that the condition comes from him, and Paul says death spread to all "because all have sinned" (Rom 5:12). Each dies for his own sin, as Ezekiel says. And Ezekiel himself charges a whole house with one hard heart (3:7), and tells his hearers they defile themselves "after the manner of your ancestors" (20:30), which he counts as their own sin.

Two other texts are offered on the same side. Paul says Jacob and Esau "had done nothing good or bad" before they were born (Rom 9:11). Moses speaks of children "who today do not yet know right from wrong" (Deut 1:39). Both deny deeds or knowledge. Neither denies a condition, and Paul says the condition is "by nature" (Eph 2:3).

**How it passes to each person is not stated, and this paper does not state it.** Scripture gives the source and the fact. It does not say whether the soul comes from the parents or is created in each. Those questions divide the traditions that affirm the doctrine, and the claim here does not depend on how they are answered.

**Where the condition is found.** The texts No One Comes uses place it in several parts of the man.
- The heart and the will: "they are not willing to listen to me; because all the house of Israel have a hard forehead and a stubborn heart" (Ezek 3:7).
- The mind: "the mind that is set on the flesh is hostile to God; it does not submit to God's law—indeed it cannot" (Rom 8:7).
- The understanding: "they are unable to understand them because they are spiritually discerned" (1 Cor 2:14).

Paul puts several of them in one sentence: "They are darkened in their understanding, alienated from the life of God because of their ignorance and hardness of heart" (Eph 4:18).

Gathering these into one claim, that the condition is found across these parts of the man, is a synthesis. Each text is direct about its own part. None of them says the rest.

**What is not claimed.** That every act a man performs is as evil as it could be. No One Comes grants that an unregenerate man can do civil good, keep his word, and love his children. What he does not do while the condition remains is come.

**Agreement.** The Canons of Dort (III/IV, articles 1 through 3) teach the corruption of mind, will, and affections, its spread from Adam, and the inability of the unregenerate to return to God. This paper agrees on the inability, on Adam as the source, and on the condition being native and found across the parts of the man named above. It does not agree or disagree about how the corruption spreads, since it says nothing about that.

## Unconditional election

**What is imported.** The election is to salvation. It is an antecedent determination with persons as its objects (Rom 8:29–30), made before they had done anything (Rom 9:11; 2 Tim 1:9). It is not conditioned on works, and it does not rest on the man's willing or running. No responsive candidate other than faith has a text behind it. For faith, "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man" shows only that the reading with no criterion is better supported. It leaves the step from better supported to excluded to this paper.

**The argument for faith.** It uses the imports of all four papers and adds no new premise. It runs in seven steps.

1. **Everyone God regenerates is saved.** The act claim: every instance of the regenerating act yields faith. Faith is how a man is justified: "a person is justified not by the works of the law but through faith in Jesus Christ" (Gal 2:16), and "to one who without works trusts him who justifies the ungodly, such faith is reckoned as righteousness" (Rom 4:5). And the keeping claim: no one who is justified finally falls away. So every man God regenerates believes, is justified, and is kept.

2. **God does not regenerate everyone.** Not all come to life: "those who have done good, to the resurrection of life, and those who have done evil, to the resurrection of condemnation" (John 5:29). If every man were regenerated, step 1 would bring every man to life. So some are not regenerated. The verse describes the two groups by what they have done. This step uses only that there are two.

3. **Whom God regenerates is his decision.** The regenerating act is his: "In fulfillment of his own purpose he gave us birth by the word of truth" (Jas 1:18). The Greek is βουληθεὶς ἀπεκύησεν ἡμᾶς, *boulētheis apekyēsen hēmas*, having willed, he gave us birth. And since step 2 shows that not everyone receives the act, the decision distinguishes one man from another.

4. **That decision is an election, as the imported definition defines it.** By steps 1 and 3, God's decision to regenerate a man settles that the man will believe, be justified, and be kept. Believers belong to the promise-heir class (Gal 3:29, imported). So the decision assigns the man the saving destiny of that class. And it comes before his faith, because his faith is its result. That is what "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man" defines election to be: "the antecedent divine determination by which a person is assigned the salvific destiny of the promise-class." The identification does not assume what is being proved. It shows that this decision has the object, the timing, and the result the definition names.

5. **That decision is not conditioned on the man's faith.** No One Comes shows that no man believes unless God first changes his heart. The act claim shows that the change yields faith. So the only faith any man will have is faith God gives him. To foresee a man's faith is therefore to foresee what God will do to him. A rule that elects those God foresees will believe elects those God foresees he will make believe. That rule names nothing in the man that God's decision does not put there. The same holds if what is foreseen is that the man would believe if regenerated. That is a fact about what the act does, and it is true of every man.

   The objector may put the counterfactual under a grace short of regeneration: God foresees who would freely believe if given some grace. No One Comes answers it with a fork. If the grace removes the condition Ezekiel 3:7 names, it is the change under another name, and the act claim says that act yields faith in every man who receives it, so the counterfactual is true of every man and marks no one off. If the grace leaves the condition in place, necessity says no one believes under it, so the counterfactual is false of every man and marks no one off. Either way it names nothing in the man that God's decision does not put there.

   Not Grounded counts a condition every man meets as still a condition, because a man could fail it. Faith given by this decision is not of that kind. No one who receives the act fails to believe, and no one who does not receive it believes. So faith marks no one off from anyone else apart from the decision. A man without faith lacks it because he was not given the act. He was not passed over because he lacked it.

6. **That decision is not conditioned on any earlier response either.** An objector may move the condition back: God regenerates the man who first seeks, or softens, or does not resist. No One Comes does not answer this by itself, since it denies only a saving response from the unchanged heart and grants that the unchanged man hears and does civil good. "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man" answers it. Works are excluded by name: "not by works but by his call" (Rom 9:12), and "if it is by grace, it is no longer on the basis of works" (Rom 11:6). And any other responsive candidate needs a text making it the condition of the choosing. None has been produced, so under the evidential rule none is available. That does not declare such a condition impossible. It says Scripture does not give one.

   The objector may move the condition forward instead: God elects those he foresees will persevere. That is the form the Remonstrants gave the doctrine in 1610, election of those who would believe and persevere to the end. Step 5's answer applies to it. Continuing is God's work: "it is God who is at work in you, enabling you both to will and to work" (Phil 2:13), and the keeping claim says no one who is justified finally falls away. So to foresee a man's perseverance is to foresee what God will do in him. And no text makes perseverance the condition of the choosing. The conditional texts (Col 1:23; Heb 3:14) make continuing the way to the end, which is a means of receiving and not a condition of election.

7. **Therefore the election is not conditioned on anything the man does, faith included.** Any account that places election after foreseen faith places it after a determination that has already assigned the destiny. On such an account God's choice according to foreseen faith is not the choice that decides the man's destiny. The choice that decides it is the earlier decision to regenerate him, and that decision is not conditioned on anything he does. Works and faith are excluded outright. Other responses are excluded as step 6 excludes them: Scripture gives none.

**"Confirm your call and election" — 2 Peter 1:10.** An objector will read the verse as making election depend on conduct: "be all the more eager to confirm your call and election, for if you do this, you will never stumble." The phrase is βεβαίαν ποιεῖσθαι, *bebaian poieisthai*, to make firm or sure. Peter gives the result in the readers: they will never stumble. And he has said where their capacity came from: "His divine power has given us everything needed for life and godliness" (1:3). The next verse gives what follows: "For in this way, entry into the eternal kingdom of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ will be richly provided for you" (1:11). So the readers' conduct is the way they enter the kingdom. That is a means of receiving, as the conditional texts are (step 6). What is made firm is a call and election the readers already have. The verses name nothing God's choosing depended on, so step 6's rule applies to them.

**The priority is of explanation, not of time.** Accounts that ground election in foreseen faith already put election before the believing in time. What closes them is not the order of events. It is that the foreseen believing depends on a divine decision that has already assigned the destiny.

**No import depends on this conclusion.** A reader will ask whether the argument is circular: whether the act claim or the keeping claim was itself argued from the exclusion of foreseen faith. It was not. "God Brings Every Heir to Faith" argues the act claim from the heart-gift promises and 1 John 5:1–5, and it argues the keeping claim by three routes, one of which, Romans 5:9–10, uses nothing but Romans 5. That paper imports four things from "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man" — the evidential rule, the personal predestining of Romans 8:29–30, Esau's being passed over in the designation, and the distinction between the olive tree and the Romans 8 company. None of them is the conclusion reached here.

**What is not claimed.** That no property of the man bears on his election. "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man" leaves open properties a man does not perform, such as weakness or low birth, and so does this paper. Nor does this paper say why God regenerates the ones he does. It says only that no qualifying condition of his decision is something they do. It does not deny that God has reasons and purposes of his own.

**Agreement.** The Canons of Dort (I, article 9) deny that election is made on the basis of foreseen faith, the obedience of faith, holiness, or any other good quality or disposition. This paper agrees for faith and for everything the man does. Dort's wider denial, of any good quality, reaches properties this paper leaves open.

## Irresistible grace

**What is imported.** The act claim: every instance of God's regenerating act yields faith, and the act does not fail in anyone to whom it is given. The heir claim: every heir is brought to faith by God's act. And from No One Comes, production: where the response occurs, God's act brought it about.

**What that is.** It is an outcome claim about one act. Everyone to whom God gives the regenerating act comes to faith. The word *irresistible* is not Scripture's, and "God Brings Every Heir to Faith" states the claim without it.

**What is not claimed.** That men never resist God. Stephen's hearers were "forever opposing the Holy Spirit" (Acts 7:51), and "God Brings Every Heir to Faith" grants it: the resistance is to the Spirit speaking through the prophets, not to a regenerating act given and defeated. Nor is it claimed that every approach God makes to a man — the light that "enlightens everyone" (John 1:9), the outward call, the kindness meant to lead to repentance — brings him to faith. And nothing is claimed about how the act works on the will.

**Agreement.** The Canons of Dort (III/IV, article 12) teach that all in whom God works regeneration actually believe. This paper agrees on that outcome. Dort's account of how the renewed will is moved is not taken up.

## Perseverance of the saints

**What is imported.** The keeping claim: no one who is justified finally falls away. It rests on three routes. The first runs through Romans 8: the justified are children and heirs, and the chain carries them to glory: "those whom he justified he also glorified" (Rom 8:30). The second runs through Romans 5: "Much more surely then, now that we have been justified by his blood, will we be saved through him from the wrath of God" (5:9). The third runs through Philippians: the one who began a good work "will bring it to completion by the day of Jesus Christ" (Phil 1:6), and God works in believers the willing and the working (2:13). None of the three uses another, and none uses the act claim.

**How the keeping works.** Through faith, not around it. Paul puts the command and God's working in one sentence: "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; for it is God who is at work in you, enabling you both to will and to work for his good pleasure" (Phil 2:12–13). The warnings are real, they make continuing necessary, and they are among the means by which God keeps the heirs believing. "God Brings Every Heir to Faith" places each warning it takes up under that account or under the account of a mixed assembly, and it marks which placements are readings. A reading of a warning on which a justified man is finally lost contradicts Romans 5:9–10, Philippians 1:6, and Philippians 2:13, so it is not available. That paper applies this to Hebrews 10:29 and Matthew 18:23–35.

**What is not claimed.** That a believer cannot fall *totally* for a time. The Westminster Confession (17.1) says the saints "can neither totally nor finally fall away from the state of grace." This paper claims the second. There is a text toward the first: "I have prayed for you that your own faith may not fail; and you, when once you have turned back, strengthen your brothers" (Luke 22:32). Peter denies Christ that night, and the prayer is that his faith not fail through it. That is one case, and one case does not establish the general claim.

## What is inferred, and where to attack

The imports are argued in their own papers, and an attack on one of them belongs there. What follows are the moves this paper makes.

**Step 4 of the argument for unconditional election** — that God's decision to regenerate a man is an election in the imported sense. It rests on the act claim, the keeping claim, and Galatians 3:29 carrying the regenerated into the promise-heir class. An objector who wants to break it has to show that the decision lacks one of the three things the definition names: a person as its object, a place before the person's faith, or the assignment of the saving destiny.

**Step 2** — that not everyone is regenerated. It rests on the act claim and the keeping claim together with John 5:28–29. An objector who holds that all are finally saved denies the premise, and the conclusion of step 2 does not follow for him. That position is outside the corpus reading adopted in these papers, and it would have to be argued against John 5:29 and Daniel 12:2.

**Step 5** — that foreseen faith cannot condition the decision. It rests on No One Comes (no faith without God's prior act) and the act claim (the act yields faith). An objector who wants to break it has two ways. He can produce faith God does not give, and that attack belongs in those papers. Or he can grant that God gives the faith and show a man passed over because he lacked faith, not a man who lacks faith because he was passed over. An objector who reads the act claim as a report of the cases that succeeded, and nothing more, denies that "he would believe if regenerated" holds of every man. That attack belongs in "God Brings Every Heir to Faith," step 9.

**Step 6** — that no earlier response conditions the decision, and that foreseen perseverance does not either. It rests on "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man," steps 2a and 3, not on No One Comes. For perseverance it also rests on Philippians 2:13 and the keeping claim. An objector who produces a text making a response before regeneration the condition of God's choosing has answered the evidential rule and reopened the step.

**Adam as the source of the condition.** A same-author identification of Romans 5:19 and Ephesians 2:3. An objector may hold that Romans 5 makes Adam the source of death and guilt without making him the source of the heart that will not listen, which on his reading is a later hardening. Ephesians 2:3 and Genesis 8:21 answer the *later*: the condition is there by nature and from youth. What remains contestable is only whether that native condition is part of what Romans 5:19 calls being *made sinners* through the one man. A second rival reads *made sinners* as a judicial standing, since 5:16–18 speak of condemnation and justification. That reading is available, and it is why the joining is an identification and not a direct statement. The case for it is the conjunction of Adam's act, a condition that is native and universal (Eph 2:3), and the inability No One Comes argues, not 5:19 alone. A second route does not use 5:19 at all: Romans follows the sin that entered through one man (5:12) as it rules (5:21), enslaves (6:17, 20), dwells in the flesh (7:17–18, 25), and holds the mind that cannot submit (8:3–7). An objector to that route has to show that the noun changes its referent somewhere along the chain, or that the flesh of 8:7 is not the flesh in which sin dwells at 7:18.

**The condition found across the parts of the man.** A synthesis of Ezekiel 3:7, Romans 8:7, 1 Corinthians 2:14, and Ephesians 4:18. Each is direct about its own part of the man, and Ephesians 4:18 names several together. The gathering is the move. It covers the parts these texts name; it is not a separate argument about every faculty.

---

## Not claimed here

For whom Christ died. Why God regenerates the ones he does, beyond that no qualifying condition of it is something they do. How grace acts on the will. How the condition from Adam passes to each person. That no property a man does not perform bears on his election. That a believer cannot fall totally for a time. That every act of the unregenerate is as evil as it could be.

Nor is it claimed that the man does not believe. He does, the believing is his, and it is how he receives what was promised.
