# Four Points of Calvinism: What the Papers Establish

**Claim under review.** Four claims, each about an outcome.

- **Total depravity.** No one comes to Christ unless God first changes his heart. The condition that keeps a man from coming is native to every man, Christ excepted, comes from Adam, and is found in the heart, the will, the mind, and the understanding.
- **Unconditional election.** God's election is to salvation, it has persons as its objects, it comes before anything they do, and it is not conditioned on anything they do. That includes their faith, actual or foreseen. For responses other than works and faith, the claim is that Scripture gives none as a condition. Whether something a man is, rather than something he does, bears on his election is left open.
- **Irresistible grace.** Everyone to whom God gives the regenerating act comes to faith.
- **Perseverance of the saints.** No one who is justified finally falls away.

**What this paper does.** Most of it joins conclusions already argued in four papers. It adds three things of its own. The first is the argument in the section on unconditional election, which completes the case for faith that "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man" left comparative. The other two are syntheses in the section on total depravity: Adam as the source of the condition, and the condition gathered across the parts of the man. Everything else is stated from the imports, and each synthesis is marked as one.

**How the doctrines are stated here.** Each doctrine is stated as a claim about outcomes: who can come, whom God chooses and why, who believes, and who is kept. The traditional formulations also give an account of how grace acts on the will. Scripture says that God is at work in the willing of believers — "it is God who is at work in you, enabling you both to will and to work for his good pleasure" (Phil 2:13) — but it does not say how, and this paper brackets the mechanism. The Canons of Dort and the Westminster Confession are named below only to report where the outcomes agree with them. They are not premises.

**Not under review.** For whom Christ died. Why God passes over anyone, and how many. How the will works. How the condition inherited from Adam passes to each person. Whether a property a man does not perform — something he is rather than something he does — bears on his election. Whether a believer can fall totally for a time, short of finally.

## The imports

Each import is argued in its own paper. A defect in an import is a defect in the paper that argues it, and any claim here that rests on the import falls with it.

- Believers belong to that class (steps 9 through 12).

## Total depravity

**What is imported.** No One Comes argues that no one comes to Christ unless God first changes his heart. The response does not occur while the heart Ezekiel names remains, and where the response occurs, God's act brought it about. Step 10 of that paper crosses from Ezekiel's audience to every man, on Romans 8:7–8, Ephesians 2:3, 1 Corinthians 2:14, and John 6:44 and 65. Its closing section adds that no one is righteous before God: "There is no one who is righteous, not even one" (Rom 3:10).

**The condition is native.** Paul says where it comes from in the man: "we were by nature children of wrath, like everyone else" (Eph 2:3). The word is φύσει, *physei*, by nature. Genesis says it is there from youth: "the inclination of the human heart is evil from youth" (Gen 8:21). So the condition is not merely acquired by habit or example. A man has it as he is. It can also grow worse by practice: those who would not honor God "became futile in their thinking, and their senseless minds were darkened" (Rom 1:21).

**The condition comes from Adam.** Paul names the source. "Sin came into the world through one man, and death came through sin, and so death spread to all because all have sinned" (Rom 5:12). "By the one man's disobedience the many were made sinners" (5:19). "All die in Adam" (1 Cor 15:22).

That Adam is the source of sin and death is direct statement. That he is the source of the particular condition No One Comes describes — the heart that will not listen — is a synthesis. It joins Romans 5:19's *made sinners* to Ephesians 2:3's *by nature*, and the joining is Paul's usage read across two letters.

**It does not come by copying Adam.** Death reigned "even over those whose sins were not like the transgression of Adam" (Rom 5:14), so death does not depend on repeating his act. And the many were made sinners "by the one man's disobedience" (5:19). The cause Paul names is Adam's act, not their copying of it. Neither verse says how what comes from Adam passes to each person.

**How it passes to each person is not stated, and this paper does not state it.** Scripture gives the source and the fact. It does not say whether the soul comes from the parents or is created in each. Those questions divide the traditions that affirm the doctrine, and the claim here does not depend on how they are answered.

**Where the condition is found.** The texts No One Comes uses place it in several parts of the man.
- The heart and the will: "they are not willing to listen to me; because all the house of Israel have a hard forehead and a stubborn heart" (Ezek 3:7).
- The mind: "the mind that is set on the flesh is hostile to God; it does not submit to God's law—indeed it cannot" (Rom 8:7).
- The understanding: "they are unable to understand them because they are spiritually discerned" (1 Cor 2:14).

Paul puts several of them in one sentence: "They are darkened in their understanding, alienated from the life of God because of their ignorance and hardness of heart" (Eph 4:18).
Gathering these into one claim, that the condition is found across these parts of the man, is a synthesis. Each text is direct about its own part. None of them says the rest.

**What is not claimed.** That every act a man performs is as evil as it could be. No One Comes grants that an unregenerate man can do civil good, keep his word, and love his children. What he does not do while the condition remains is come.

**Agreement.** The Canons of Dort (III/IV, articles 1 through 3) teach the corruption of mind, will, and affections, its spread from Adam, and the inability of the unregenerate to return to God. This paper agrees on the inability, on Adam as the source, and on the condition being native and found across the parts of the man named above. It does not agree or disagree about how the corruption spreads, since it says nothing about that.

## Unconditional election

**What is imported.** The election is to salvation. It is an antecedent determination with persons as its objects (Rom 8:29–30), made before they had done anything (Rom 9:11; 2 Tim 1:9). It is not conditioned on works, and it does not rest on the man's willing or running. No responsive candidate other than faith has a text behind it. For faith, "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man" shows only that the reading with no criterion is better supported. It leaves the step from better supported to excluded to this paper.

**The argument for faith.** It uses the imports of all four papers and adds no new premise. It runs in seven steps.

1. **Everyone God regenerates is saved.** The act claim: every instance of the regenerating act yields faith. Faith is how a man is justified: "a person is justified not by the works of the law but through faith in Jesus Christ" (Gal 2:16), and "to one who without works trusts him who justifies the ungodly, such faith is reckoned as righteousness" (Rom 4:5). And the keeping claim: no one who is justified finally falls away. So every man God regenerates believes, is justified, and is kept.

2. **God does not regenerate everyone.** Not all come to life: "those who have done good, to the resurrection of life, and those who have done evil, to the resurrection of condemnation" (John 5:29). If every man were regenerated, step 1 would bring every man to life. So some are not regenerated. The verse describes the two groups by what they have done. This step uses only that there are two.

3. **Whom God regenerates is his decision.** The regenerating act is his: "In fulfillment of his own purpose he gave us birth by the word of truth" (Jas 1:18). The Greek is βουληθεὶς ἀπεκύησεν ἡμᾶς, *boulētheis apekyēsen hēmas*, having willed, he gave us birth. And since step 2 shows that not everyone receives the act, the decision distinguishes one man from another.

4. **That decision is an election, as the imported definition defines it.** By steps 1 and 3, God's decision to regenerate a man settles that the man will believe, be justified, and be kept. Believers belong to the promise-heir class (Gal 3:29, imported). So the decision assigns the man the saving destiny of that class. And it comes before his faith, because his faith is its result. That is what "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man" defines election to be: "the antecedent divine determination by which a person is assigned the salvific destiny of the promise-class." The identification does not assume what is being proved. It shows that this decision has the object, the timing, and the result the definition names.

5. **That decision is not conditioned on the man's faith.** No One Comes shows that no man believes unless God first changes his heart. The act claim shows that the change yields faith. So the only faith any man will have is faith God gives him. To foresee a man's faith is therefore to foresee what God will do to him. A rule that elects those God foresees will believe elects those God foresees he will make believe. That rule names nothing in the man that God's decision does not put there. The same holds if what is foreseen is that the man would believe if regenerated. That is a fact about what the act does, and it is true of every man.

6. **That decision is not conditioned on any earlier response either.** An objector may move the condition back: God regenerates the man who first seeks, or softens, or does not resist. No One Comes does not answer this by itself, since it denies only a saving response from the unchanged heart and grants that the unchanged man hears and does civil good. "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man" answers it. Works are excluded by name: "not by works but by his call" (Rom 9:12), and "if it is by grace, it is no longer on the basis of works" (Rom 11:6). And any other responsive candidate needs a text making it the condition of the choosing. None has been produced, so under the evidential rule none is available. That does not declare such a condition impossible. It says Scripture does not give one.

7. **Therefore the election is not conditioned on anything the man does, faith included.** Any account that places election after foreseen faith places it after a determination that has already assigned the destiny. On such an account God's choice according to foreseen faith is not the choice that decides the man's destiny. The choice that decides it is the earlier decision to regenerate him, and that decision is not conditioned on anything he does. Works and faith are excluded outright. Other responses are excluded as step 6 excludes them: Scripture gives none.

**The priority is of explanation, not of time.** Accounts that ground election in foreseen faith already put election before the believing in time. What closes them is not the order of events. It is that the foreseen believing depends on a divine decision that has already assigned the destiny.

**No import depends on this conclusion.** A reader will ask whether the argument is circular: whether the act claim or the keeping claim was itself argued from the exclusion of foreseen faith. It was not. "God Brings Every Heir to Faith" argues the act claim from the heart-gift promises and 1 John 5:1–5, and it argues the keeping claim by two routes, one of which, Romans 5:9–10, uses nothing but Romans 5. That paper imports three things from "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man" — the evidential rule, the personal predestining of Romans 8:29–30, and the distinction between the olive tree and the Romans 8 company. None of them is the conclusion reached here.

**What is not claimed.** That no property of the man bears on his election. "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man" leaves open properties a man does not perform, such as weakness or low birth, and so does this paper. Nor does this paper say why God regenerates the ones he does. It says only that no qualifying condition of his decision is something they do. It does not deny that God has reasons and purposes of his own.

**Agreement.** The Canons of Dort (I, article 9) deny that election is made on the basis of foreseen faith, the obedience of faith, holiness, or any other good quality or disposition. This paper agrees for faith and for everything the man does. Dort's wider denial, of any good quality, reaches properties this paper leaves open.

## Irresistible grace

**What is imported.** The act claim: every instance of God's regenerating act yields faith, and the act does not fail in anyone to whom it is given. The heir claim: every heir is brought to faith by God's act. And from No One Comes, production: where the response occurs, God's act brought it about.

**What that is.** It is an outcome claim about one act. Everyone to whom God gives the regenerating act comes to faith. The word *irresistible* is not Scripture's, and "God Brings Every Heir to Faith" states the claim without it.

**What is not claimed.** That men never resist God. Stephen's hearers were "forever opposing the Holy Spirit" (Acts 7:51), and "God Brings Every Heir to Faith" grants it: the resistance is to the Spirit speaking through the prophets, not to a regenerating act given and defeated. Nor is it claimed that every approach God makes to a man — the light that "enlightens everyone" (John 1:9), the outward call, the kindness meant to lead to repentance — brings him to faith. And nothing is claimed about how the act works on the will.

**Agreement.** The Canons of Dort (III/IV, article 12) teach that all in whom God works regeneration actually believe. This paper agrees on that outcome. Dort's account of how the renewed will is moved is not taken up.

## Perseverance of the saints

**What is imported.** The keeping claim: no one who is justified finally falls away. It rests on two routes. The first runs through Romans 8: the justified are children and heirs, and the chain carries them to glory: "those whom he justified he also glorified" (Rom 8:30). The second runs through Romans 5: "Much more surely then, now that we have been justified by his blood, will we be saved through him from the wrath of God" (5:9). The second route uses neither the first nor the act claim.

**How the keeping works.** Through faith, not around it. The warnings are real, they make continuing necessary, and they are among the means by which God keeps the heirs believing. "God Brings Every Heir to Faith" places each warning it takes up under that account or under the account of a mixed assembly, and it marks which placements are readings. Hebrews 10:29 and Matthew 18:23–35 are weighed there, not closed.

**What is not claimed.** That a believer cannot fall *totally* for a time. The Westminster Confession (17.1) says the saints "can neither totally nor finally fall away from the state of grace." This paper claims the second. There is a text toward the first: "I have prayed for you that your own faith may not fail; and you, when once you have turned back, strengthen your brothers" (Luke 22:32). Peter denies Christ that night, and the prayer is that his faith not fail through it. That is one case, and one case does not establish the general claim.

## What is inferred, and where to attack

The imports are argued in their own papers, and an attack on one of them belongs there. What follows are the moves this paper makes.

**Step 4 of the argument for unconditional election** — that God's decision to regenerate a man is an election in the imported sense. It rests on the act claim, the keeping claim, and Galatians 3:29 carrying the regenerated into the promise-heir class. An objector who wants to break it has to show that the decision lacks one of the three things the definition names: a person as its object, a place before the person's faith, or the assignment of the saving destiny.

**Step 2** — that not everyone is regenerated. It rests on the act claim and the keeping claim together with John 5:28–29. An objector who holds that all are finally saved denies the premise, and the conclusion of step 2 does not follow for him. That position is outside the corpus reading adopted in these papers, and it would have to be argued against John 5:29 and Daniel 12:2.

**Step 5** — that foreseen faith cannot condition the decision. It rests on No One Comes (no faith without God's prior act) and the act claim (the act yields faith). An objector who wants to break it has to produce faith God does not give, and that attack belongs in those papers. An objector who reads the act claim as a report of the cases that succeeded, and nothing more, denies that "he would believe if regenerated" holds of every man. That attack belongs in "God Brings Every Heir to Faith," step 9.

**Step 6** — that no earlier response conditions the decision. It rests on "The Election Is Not Grounded in the Man," steps 2a and 3, not on No One Comes. An objector who produces a text making a response before regeneration the condition of God's choosing has answered the evidential rule and reopened the step.

**Adam as the source of the condition.** A synthesis of Romans 5:19 and Ephesians 2:3, both Paul. An objector may hold that Romans 5 makes Adam the source of death and guilt without making him the source of the heart that will not listen, which on his reading is a later hardening. Ephesians 2:3 and Genesis 8:21 answer the *later*: the condition is there by nature and from youth. What remains contestable is only whether that native condition is part of what Romans 5:19 calls being *made sinners* through the one man. A second rival reads *made sinners* as a judicial standing, since 5:16–18 speak of condemnation and justification. That reading is available, and it is why the joining is synthesis. The case for it is the conjunction of Adam's act, a condition that is native and universal (Eph 2:3), and the inability No One Comes argues, not 5:19 alone.

**The condition found across the parts of the man.** A synthesis of Ezekiel 3:7, Romans 8:7, 1 Corinthians 2:14, and Ephesians 4:18. Each is direct about its own part of the man, and Ephesians 4:18 names several together. The gathering is the move. It covers the parts these texts name; it is not a separate argument about every faculty.

---

## Not claimed here

For whom Christ died. Why God regenerates the ones he does, beyond that no qualifying condition of it is something they do. How grace acts on the will. How the condition from Adam passes to each person. That no property a man does not perform bears on his election. That a believer cannot fall totally for a time. That every act of the unregenerate is as evil as it could be.

Nor is it claimed that the man does not believe. He does, the believing is his, and it is how he receives what was promised.
